        Currencies

        The quote currency is the one you buy the holding in. The economic currency here is the currency of the country where the company is based (for ETFs, of the companies they hold). A US stock bought in Milan in euros is still converted from the dollar. Where the company earns its revenue does not count: a European company that sells mostly in Asia still counts as euro.

        Dependencies between holdings

        Correlation runs from -1 (they move in opposite directions) to +1 (they move together) and is computed on weekly euro returns over the last two years. Several highly correlated holdings behave like a single position.

        Risk and scenarios

        Weight and risk of each holding

        Above the diagonal, a holding carries more risk than its weight, because it swings a lot or because it moves with the rest of the portfolio. Hover over or tap a dot for details.

        Today's holdings over the last two years: two ways of counting

        A hindsight simulation with the holdings chosen today, so it is neither your return nor a forecast. Today's weights, fixed: rebalances every week to today's weights; since the weights are high precisely in the holdings that have already risen, it counts the same gain twice and overstates. Today's quantities, unchanged: keeps the same number of shares for two years without rebalancing (a holding listed later counts as cash until its first price).

        Scenarios

        Estimated effect of a market move, from the average sensitivity of the last two years. Each scenario includes the typical stock-market move that comes with it, so they do not add up. The shocks differ in size: compare the figures, not the bars.
